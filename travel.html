Franklin Falls Trail is an easy, family-friendly out-and-back hike in the Mount Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest near Snoqualmie Pass, roughly 2 miles round trip with about 337 feet of elevation gain, taking about 1 to 1.5 hours to complete. The trail follows at 0.7 miles in, the trail runs parallel to National Forest Road 58 briefly before reaching the falls about 0.3 miles further along, with the standout site being Franklin Falls itself, a 70-foot drop that's actually the last of three tiers totaling 135 feet, best seen with strong flows between April and July; there's also a short connecting side trail to the historic 1860s Snoqualmie Pass Wagon Road about a quarter mile from the falls, which some hikers use to make a small loop back. It's a hugely popular, well-maintained, mostly shaded path along the South Fork Snoqualmie River — great for kids or anyone short on time — though the final stretch of rock steps down to the falls' splash pool can get slick, so sturdy shoes matter, and arriving early is wise since the trailhead parking lot fills up fast.

Mount St. Helens Summit via Ptarmigan Trail — This is a strenuous, full-day climb up an active volcano in southwest Washington, covering roughly 8.6–8.8 miles round trip with about 4,600–4,650 feet of elevation gain, typically taking 7 to 12 hours depending on pace. The route starts at the Climbers' Bivouac trailhead, following the Ptarmigan Trail for the first 2.1 miles as it climbs about 1,000 feet through forest and open meadows up to the Loowit Trail junction — a scenic standalone hike in its own right. Beyond that point, a climbing permit is required to continue up Monitor Ridge, where the next 2,500 vertical feet crosses ash-dusted boulder fields, followed by a final 1,000-foot slog through loose ash and rock to the crater rim, often described as "two steps forward, one step back." The reward is dramatic, raw volcanic geology and sweeping views in every direction from the rim of the crater created by the famous 1980 eruption, with sightlines to Mount Adams, Mount Hood, and Mount Rainier on clear days; it's best attempted between late spring and early fall when the route is mostly snow-free.

Granite Mountain Trail — A tough but hugely popular Snoqualmie Pass classic, this out-and-back trail covers about 8 miles with roughly 3,700 feet of elevation gain, generally taking most hikers 4 to 6 hours. The hike begins at the Pratt Lake trailhead through cool, mossy forest, then after 1.2 miles splits off onto the steeper, rockier Granite Mountain Trail, climbing at nearly 1,000 feet of gain per mile through switchbacks and an avalanche chute crossing. The payoff is sweeping alpine views of pointed Kaleetan Peak, the deep-blue Crystal Lake, the climber-dotted Tooth, and Mount Rainier dominating the horizon to the south, culminating at a historic fire lookout cabin/tower at the summit that's been staffed by volunteers since 1924. Because part of the route crosses avalanche terrain, it's best done in summer or with proper avalanche gear and awareness in winter/spring.

Silver Falls Trail (Mount Rainier) — This is an easy-to-moderate, family-friendly loop in the southeast corner of Mount Rainier National Park, covering about 2.8–2.9 miles with roughly 518–750 feet of elevation gain, usually taking around 1.5 hours. Starting from the Ohanapecosh Visitor Center, the trail passes the site of an old hot springs resort and big old-growth trees before crossing Laughingwater Creek, weaving through classic mossy Pacific Northwest forest of Douglas fir and western red cedar. The centerpiece is Silver Falls itself, where the Ohanapecosh River careens over a series of ledges before plunging about 40 feet into a deep blue pool, with a footbridge and multiple viewpoints offering great photo spots of both the falls and the rapids above them. It's a lower-elevation, gentle-gradient hike that also works well as an early or late-season option when higher trails are still snowed in.
